# Graph Repository

A running collection of standalone, interactive graphs. Each graph is a single
self-contained HTML file (built with the `econ-chart-style` house chart
engine) that can be opened directly from GitHub Pages, embedded, or downloaded
as a PNG.

Every graph gets its own row in the table below and, where the underlying
analysis required more than reading a published table off the shelf, its own
subfolder with the source data and the script used to compute the numbers.

## Repo layout

```
<graph-name>.html                        the live, self-contained chart (open this to view/interact/download PNG)
<graph-name>/
  data/                                  raw source data as downloaded from the original agency/publisher
  scripts/                               script(s) that reproduce the chart's numbers from the raw data
```

Only graphs that required real computation (not just plotting numbers already
published in a table) get a subfolder — a graph built straight from a
published series may just be the `.html` file on its own.

## Social Security by income quintile

- **Live page:** https://econchrisclarke.github.io/Graph-Repository/social-security-by-income-quintile.html
- **Data:** [`social-security-by-income-quintile/data/cbo_61911_supplemental_data.xlsx`](social-security-by-income-quintile/data/cbo_61911_supplemental_data.xlsx) — CBO's *The Distribution of Household Income, 2022* (January 2026), Supplemental Data workbook, as published at [cbo.gov/publication/61911](https://www.cbo.gov/publication/61911).
- **Script:** [`social-security-by-income-quintile/scripts/compute_ss_shares.py`](social-security-by-income-quintile/scripts/compute_ss_shares.py) — reads Tables 1 and 5 of the workbook and computes each income quintile's share of aggregate Social Security benefits. Run with `python scripts/compute_ss_shares.py` from inside the `social-security-by-income-quintile/` folder.
- **Methodology summary:** households are ranked into equal-population fifths by CBO's "income before transfers and taxes" (market income plus Social Security, Medicare, unemployment insurance, and workers' compensation), adjusted for household size. Each quintile's share of total Social Security benefits is its average benefit per household (Table 5) times its household count (Table 1), as a share of the sum across all five quintiles. Full detail is in the script's docstring and in the chart's own footnote.

## China and the Solow model: growth since reaching $15,000

- **Live page:** https://econchrisclarke.github.io/Graph-Repository/china-solow-growth-since-15k.html ([file in repo](china-solow-growth-since-15k.html))
- **Question:** is China's slowdown in GDP per capita growth what the Solow model would lead us to expect at this income level? This first step is descriptive: it lines China up against 26 other economies by years since each reached $15,000 GDP per capita, so growth is compared at the same stage of development rather than the same calendar year. It is not yet a test of the Solow model's predictions.
- **Data:** [`china-solow-growth-since-15k/data/owid_maddison_gdp_per_capita.csv`](china-solow-growth-since-15k/data/owid_maddison_gdp_per_capita.csv) — GDP per capita from the Maddison Project Database 2023 (2011 international dollars), as republished by [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/gdp-per-capita-maddison-project-database). The original release is at [rug.nl/ggdc/historicaldevelopment/maddison](https://www.rug.nl/ggdc/historicaldevelopment/maddison/releases/maddison-project-database-2023). Derived series: [`growth_since_15k.csv`](china-solow-growth-since-15k/data/growth_since_15k.csv).
- **Scripts:** [`china-solow-growth-since-15k/scripts/`](china-solow-growth-since-15k/scripts/) — `prep.py` computes each country's crossing year and growth; `mkchart.py` builds the chart config; `tools/` holds the chart builder and (modified) chart template. Run `python prep.py`, then `python mkchart.py`, then `python tools/build_chart.py --config ../data/chart.json --template tools/chart-template.html --out ../../china-solow-growth-since-15k.html` from inside the `scripts/` folder.
- **Methodology summary:** year 0 for a country is the first year after which its GDP per capita stays at or above $15,000 (China: 2017). The vertical axis is the trailing 5-year compound annual growth rate, `(GDPpc_t / GDPpc_{t-5})^(1/5) - 1`. The 26 comparison economies were chosen by hand (advanced economies plus East Asian, Southern and Eastern European and Latin American catch-up cases); oil states, tiny economies, successor states and countries that fell back below $15,000 are excluded. The black median line is taken across the comparison countries with data at each x value, so its country mix shifts at longer horizons. China has only five years of data past $15,000 (through 2022).

## China and the Solow model: growth against income level

- **Live page:** https://econchrisclarke.github.io/Graph-Repository/china-solow-growth-vs-income.html ([file in repo](china-solow-growth-vs-income.html))
- **Question:** the companion to the [growth-since-$15,000 chart](china-solow-growth-since-15k/): instead of years since reaching $15,000, the horizontal axis is GDP per capita itself (log scale, $5k to $85k). Each line is one economy traced through income space; China is in red and the black line is the cross-country median at each income level. It is descriptive, not yet a test of the Solow model.
- **Data:** [`china-solow-growth-vs-income/data/owid_maddison_gdp_per_capita.csv`](china-solow-growth-vs-income/data/owid_maddison_gdp_per_capita.csv) — GDP per capita from the Maddison Project Database 2023 (2011 international dollars), as republished by [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/gdp-per-capita-maddison-project-database); original release at [rug.nl/ggdc](https://www.rug.nl/ggdc/historicaldevelopment/maddison/releases/maddison-project-database-2023). Derived series: [`growth_vs_income.csv`](china-solow-growth-vs-income/data/growth_vs_income.csv).
- **Scripts:** [`china-solow-growth-vs-income/scripts/`](china-solow-growth-vs-income/scripts/) — `prep_income.py` computes growth and keeps observations from 1955 with GDP per capita of at least $5,000 (and asserts that no year in any window is interpolated); `mkchart_income.py` computes the median and builds the chart config; `tools/` holds the chart builder and template. From inside `scripts/`: `python prep_income.py`, `python mkchart_income.py`, then `python tools/build_chart.py --config ../data/chart_income.json --template tools/chart-template.html --out ../../china-solow-growth-vs-income.html`.
- **Methodology summary:** growth is the trailing 5-year compound annual rate ending in each year, `(GDPpc_t / GDPpc_{t-5})^(1/5) - 1`, plotted against GDP per capita in year t. The median is built by cutting income into log-spaced bands about 15% wide, averaging each country's growth within a band, and taking the median across countries (each country counts once), shown only where at least 8 countries are in the band. The 26 comparison economies are the same hand-picked set as the growth-since-$15,000 chart, all of which eventually passed $15,000; that leaves out countries that stayed poor, so the median is biased upward at lower incomes. High-income bands contain only recent decades, so the country mix shifts along the axis.

## Used merchandise stores as a share of GAFO sales

- **Live page:** https://econchrisclarke.github.io/Graph-Repository/used-merchandise-share-of-gafo.html ([file in repo](used-merchandise-share-of-gafo.html))
- **Data:** [`used-merchandise-share-of-gafo/data/mrtssales92-present.xlsx`](used-merchandise-share-of-gafo/data/mrtssales92-present.xlsx) — Census Bureau, Monthly Retail Trade Survey, *Estimates of Monthly Retail and Food Services Sales by Kind of Business*, 1992 to present, as published at [census.gov/retail/sales.html](https://www.census.gov/retail/sales.html). Derived series: [`used_merch_gafo_monthly.csv`](used-merchandise-share-of-gafo/data/used_merch_gafo_monthly.csv).
- **Scripts:** [`used-merchandise-share-of-gafo/scripts/`](used-merchandise-share-of-gafo/scripts/) — `prep.py` reads the not-seasonally-adjusted used merchandise (NAICS 4533) and GAFO series from the workbook and computes the shares; `mkchart.py` builds the chart config; `tools/` holds the chart builder and template. From inside `scripts/`: `python prep.py`, `python mkchart.py`, then `python tools/build_chart.py --config ../data/chart.json --template tools/chart-template.html --out ../../used-merchandise-share-of-gafo.html`.
- **Methodology summary:** GAFO (general merchandise, apparel and accessories, furniture, and other) is the Census grouping of NAICS 442, 443, 448, 451, 452 and 4532, i.e. stores selling department-store-type goods; used merchandise stores (4533) are not part of it. The charted share is monthly used merchandise sales divided by monthly GAFO sales, both in nominal dollars. The data are not seasonally adjusted, so the share dips every December when GAFO holiday sales peak; the derived CSV also carries a trailing 12-month version that removes this. September 2026 is the highest monthly share in the series. Online-only resellers are classified as nonstore retailers, not in 4533. The latest month is a preliminary estimate.

## Corruption perceptions against GDP per capita (PPP)

- **Live page:** https://econchrisclarke.github.io/Graph-Repository/corruption-vs-gdp-per-capita.html ([file in repo](corruption-vs-gdp-per-capita.html))
- **Data:** [`corruption-vs-gdp-per-capita/data/CPI2025_Results.xlsx`](corruption-vs-gdp-per-capita/data/CPI2025_Results.xlsx) — Transparency International, *Corruption Perceptions Index 2025* results workbook, as published at [transparency.org/en/cpi/2025](https://www.transparency.org/en/cpi/2025). [`wb_gdp_ppp.json`](corruption-vs-gdp-per-capita/data/wb_gdp_ppp.json) — World Bank, World Development Indicators, GDP per capita, PPP (constant 2021 international $), series `NY.GDP.PCAP.PP.KD`, 2020–2025, from the [World Bank API](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/all/indicator/NY.GDP.PCAP.PP.KD?format=json&per_page=20000&date=2020:2025) (last updated 2026-11-11); [`wb_countries.json`](corruption-vs-gdp-per-capita/data/wb_countries.json) is the World Bank country list, used to drop regional aggregates. Derived series: [`cpi_vs_gdp_ppp.csv`](corruption-vs-gdp-per-capita/data/cpi_vs_gdp_ppp.csv).
- **Scripts:** [`corruption-vs-gdp-per-capita/scripts/`](corruption-vs-gdp-per-capita/scripts/) — `prep.py` reads the CPI 2025 scores (parsing the workbook's strict-OOXML XML directly, since openpyxl cannot read it) and joins them to the latest World Bank GDP per capita by ISO3 code; `mkchart.py` builds the chart config; `tools/` holds the chart builder and template. The template has one change from the house version: on a log x axis, a tick label is only edge-aligned if it would otherwise spill past the plot, so the last tick ($100k) no longer collides with $50k on the vertical frame. From inside `scripts/`: `python prep.py`, `python mkchart.py`, then `python tools/build_chart.py --config ../data/chart.json --template tools/chart-template.html --out ../../corruption-vs-gdp-per-capita.html`.
- **Methodology summary:** each dot is one of the 174 countries scored in CPI 2025 that also has World Bank GDP per capita (PPP) for 2025, or 2024 for Afghanistan, the Bahamas, Lebanon and the UAE. Cuba, Eritrea, North Korea, South Sudan, Syria, Taiwan, Venezuela and Yemen are scored by TI but have no World Bank GDP data since 2023 and are left out. Kosovo's TI code (KSV) is mapped to the World Bank's (XKX). The CPI scores perceived public-sector corruption from 0 (highly corrupt) to 100 (very clean). GDP is on a log scale; the dashed line is an OLS fit of the CPI score on log10 GDP per capita (R² ≈ 0.50, slope ≈ 26 points per tenfold rise in income, n = 174). The relationship is a correlation and says nothing about which way causation runs.

## Control of corruption (World Bank WGI) against GDP per capita (PPP)

A companion to the CPI chart above, using the same GDP data, x axis and country labels.

- **Live page:** https://econchrisclarke.github.io/Graph-Repository/corruption-wgi-vs-gdp-per-capita.html ([file in repo](corruption-wgi-vs-gdp-per-capita.html))
- **Data:** [`corruption-wgi-vs-gdp-per-capita/data/wgi_cc_score.json`](corruption-wgi-vs-gdp-per-capita/data/wgi_cc_score.json) — World Bank, Worldwide Governance Indicators (2026 update, last updated 2026-09-25), Control of Corruption governance score, 0–100, series `GOV_WGI_CC.SC`, 2018–2025, from the [World Bank API](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/all/indicator/GOV_WGI_CC.SC?format=json&per_page=20000&date=2018:2025). The estimate on the indicator's original scale, roughly −2.5 to +2.5 (`GOV_WGI_CC.EST`), is kept alongside it for reference. GDP per capita, PPP (`NY.GDP.PCAP.PP.KD`) and the country list come from the same API. Derived series: [`wgi_vs_gdp_ppp.csv`](corruption-wgi-vs-gdp-per-capita/data/wgi_vs_gdp_ppp.csv).
- **Scripts:** [`corruption-wgi-vs-gdp-per-capita/scripts/`](corruption-wgi-vs-gdp-per-capita/scripts/) — `prep.py` joins the 2025 score to GDP per capita; `mkchart.py` builds the chart config and swaps World Bank names for plainer ones ("Korea, Rep." → "South Korea"); `tools/` holds the builder and the same patched template as the CPI chart. From inside `scripts/`: `python prep.py`, `python mkchart.py`, then `python tools/build_chart.py --config ../data/chart.json --template tools/chart-template.html --out ../../corruption-wgi-vs-gdp-per-capita.html`.
- **Methodology summary:** 193 economies, including territories such as Hong Kong, Macao, Bermuda and Puerto Rico that the WGI scores. GDP per capita is for 2025, or the latest year since 2023 where 2025 is missing (listed in the chart note). Economies with no GDP figure since 2023 are left out. The indicator is the World Bank's combination of about 30 surveys of firms, households and experts, rescaled to 0–100 (higher = better control of corruption). The dashed line is an OLS fit on log10 GDP per capita: R² ≈ 0.52, slope ≈ 28 points per tenfold rise in income, n = 193.

## Political corruption (V-Dem) against GDP per capita (PPP)

A second companion to the CPI chart, using the same GDP data, x axis and country labels. V-Dem's index runs the other way: higher means more corrupt.

- **Live page:** https://econchrisclarke.github.io/Graph-Repository/corruption-vdem-vs-gdp-per-capita.html ([file in repo](corruption-vdem-vs-gdp-per-capita.html))
- **Data:** [`corruption-vdem-vs-gdp-per-capita/data/owid_vdem_political_corruption.csv`](corruption-vdem-vs-gdp-per-capita/data/owid_vdem_political_corruption.csv) — V-Dem Political Corruption Index (`v2x_corr`), central estimate, from V-Dem dataset v16 (March 2026), as republished by [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/political-corruption-index); OWID's metadata is saved alongside it. GDP per capita, PPP and the country list come from the World Bank API, the same files as the CPI chart. Derived series: [`vdem_vs_gdp_ppp.csv`](corruption-vdem-vs-gdp-per-capita/data/vdem_vs_gdp_ppp.csv).
- **Scripts:** [`corruption-vdem-vs-gdp-per-capita/scripts/`](corruption-vdem-vs-gdp-per-capita/scripts/) — `prep.py` joins the 2025 score to GDP per capita on ISO3 code, mapping OWID's Kosovo code (`OWID_KOS`) to the World Bank's (`XKX`); `mkchart.py` builds the chart config; `tools/` holds the builder and the same patched template. From inside `scripts/`: `python prep.py`, `python mkchart.py`, then `python tools/build_chart.py --config ../data/chart.json --template tools/chart-template.html --out ../../corruption-vdem-vs-gdp-per-capita.html`.
- **Methodology summary:** 167 countries. The index averages V-Dem's expert-coded measures of bribery and embezzlement in the executive, legislature, judiciary and public sector, on a 0–1 scale where higher is more corrupt. Taiwan, Somaliland and Zanzibar have no World Bank GDP series, Palestine is left out because V-Dem scores Gaza and the West Bank separately, and countries with no GDP figure since 2023 are dropped. The dashed line is an OLS fit on log10 GDP per capita: R² ≈ 0.43, slope ≈ −0.39 per tenfold rise in income, n = 167. The fit is weaker than for the CPI or WGI: rich democracies pile up near zero, while middle-income countries spread across almost the whole scale.
